TITLE: Biological Diversity Act 2002 - access and benefit sharing
SOURCE: https://nbaindia.org
TYPE: law
TOPIC: traditional_knowledge

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 regulates access to India's biological resources and associated traditional knowledge and provides for fair and equitable sharing of benefits. It is administered by the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), State Biodiversity Boards and local Biodiversity Management Committees.

A foreign national, non-resident Indian or foreign-controlled company needs prior approval of the NBA to obtain Indian biological resources for research or commercial use, or to transfer research results. Applying for an intellectual property right on an invention based on Indian biological resources also needs NBA approval, which for patents can be obtained before the patent is granted.

Indian citizens and Indian companies generally must give prior intimation to the concerned State Biodiversity Board before commercial use of certain biological resources. Local communities and traditional practitioners such as vaids and hakims are exempt for local medical use.

Benefits can be shared through royalties, joint ownership of IP, technology transfer or fees, as decided by the authority. The 2023 amendment relaxed some requirements, for example by exempting codified traditional knowledge and registered AYUSH practitioners from benefit sharing, so check the current text before relying on this summary.
